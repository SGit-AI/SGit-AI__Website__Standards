The reading

It is a dual obligation. Suspension without notification is not compliance, and notification without suspension is not either.

Two acts are required of a deployer who identifies a relevant condition, and they are required together. That is not an unusual drafting pattern in EU law, and it is precisely the pattern that a compliance checklist destroys: one row, one tick, and no way to record that an organisation did the operationally obvious half and skipped the one that involves telling somebody outside.

Modelled as a graph rather than a row, the two obligations are separate nodes with separate evidence requirements, and an assessment can be partially grounded — which is the honest state most organisations are actually in.

LimbWhat evidence would ground itTypical state
Suspend useAn operational record showing the system was actually stopped, with a timestampUsually evidenced — it is a visible operational event
NotifyA record of the notification, to whom, when, and what it containedFrequently unanswered. This is the half a checklist loses

Where this is used

This provision is one of five in the worked example, and it is the one that produces a finding of a different kind from the arithmetic one — a finding about a capability that has never been exercised rather than a number that is too small.
